Heat pump for a UK rental or short-let: the £7,500 grant won't fix a 3.3-to-1 price gap

The break-even rule, the VAT holiday that flatters this winter's prices, and the two deadlines landlords and hosts in England and Wales should know.

In short: At Ofgem's October to December 2026 cap, a heat pump on mains gas saves about £48 a year and needs an SPF above 2.77 to break even. The £7,500 grant pays back mainly against LPG or electric heating, and the £9,000 uplift and 0% VAT both end on 31 March 2027.
Contents

On a mains-gas rental or short-let in England or Wales, a heat pump saves about £48 a year in fuel at the Ofgem price cap, and it needs a seasonal performance factor (SPF) of at least 2.77 just to break even with a gas boiler. The £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant therefore does not pay itself back from bills on gas; it pays back when the heat pump replaces LPG, oil or electric heating, and two deadlines expire on 31 March 2027. For a host deciding this winter, the order of questions is: what am I replacing, and what will electricity cost after the VAT holiday ends?

Key numbers (checked 20 November 2026)

  • Ofgem's price cap from 1 October to 31 December 2026 is 26.32p per kWh for electricity and 7.97p per kWh for gas, a ratio of 3.30 to 1.
  • The Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays £7,500 towards an air-to-water heat pump, or £9,000 in eligible off-gas-grid properties until 31 March 2027, according to Ofgem.
  • Electricity carries no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, and installing a heat pump is zero-rated for VAT until the same date.

Does a heat pump beat gas at 26.32p against 7.97p? Only above an SPF of 2.77

The rule is one line. A heat pump is cheaper to run than a gas boiler when electricity price ÷ gas price is lower than SPF ÷ boiler efficiency. The SPF is the heat delivered for each unit of electricity over a whole year. With Ofgem's October to December 2026 unit rates, the ratio is 26.32 ÷ 7.97 = 3.30. Taking an 84% efficient boiler (the typical fossil-fuel figure the Heat Pump Association cites from a government impact assessment), the break-even SPF is 3.30 × 0.84 = 2.77.

Here is the worked example. UK Energy Partnership estimates around £830 a year of heating electricity at 26.32p, assuming an SPF of 3, which implies about 3,153 kWh of electricity and 9,460 kWh of heat. Gas for the same heat at 84% efficiency costs 9,460 ÷ 0.84 × 7.97p = £898 a year, before standing charges.

If you also remove the gas connection, you stop paying the 29.68p daily gas standing charge, about £108 a year. At an SPF of 2.93 that lifts the saving to roughly £156. Dividing the £5,408 left after the grant (the £12,908 median cost below, minus £7,500) by £48 gives more than a century of payback on fuel alone. On gas, a heat pump is a decision about replacing a boiler, not about bills.

Why the price gap looks narrower than in the summer, and when it widens again

Ofgem's announcement of 26 August 2026 says the government removed VAT from electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, while gas still carries 5% VAT. Ofgem warns that costs cannot be compared directly with previous periods. In the July to September 2026 cap, electricity was 26.11p and gas 7.33p, a ratio of 3.56; this quarter's 3.30 is lower largely because of the VAT holiday, with gas wholesale prices also rising. Our arithmetic: if VAT of 5% returned on the same pre-VAT price, electricity would cost about 27.64p, the ratio would be 3.47, and the break-even SPF would rise to 2.91. At an SPF of 2.93 the saving over gas would shrink to about £5 a year.

Ofgem will announce the cap for 1 January to 31 March 2027 on 25 November 2026, and the cap for April to June on 23 February 2027. Rerun the one-line rule with each new pair of unit rates.

What do installed heat pumps achieve: 293% on average, but only 8% reach 3.5?

The Electrification of Heat demonstration project, funded by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero, monitored 742 heat pump systems. According to the Heat Pump Association's summary of 19 December 2024 (an industry body, and a source more than 12 months old), the sampled air source heat pumps averaged 293% efficiency, an SPF of about 2.93, against 84% for a typical fossil-fuel system. The same release cites a separate DESNZ-funded analysis of 1,717 installations between 2017 and 2022, in which 8% of air source heat pumps achieved an SPF of 3.5 or above, and it notes a gap between design figures and in-situ performance.

For a host, the lesson is to price your decision at 2.9, not at the brochure's 4. A heat pump on a short-let that sits empty and cold between bookings may also behave differently from the monitored family homes. Ask the installer for the design flow temperature, the SPF estimate under the MCS pre-sale calculation, and radiator sizes room by room.

Where the £7,500 pays back: LPG, oil and electric heating

UK Energy Partnership reproduces Energy Saving Trust estimates (July 2026 fuel prices, three-bedroom semi, standard tariff): replacing an efficient gas boiler ranges from about £110 a year more to £220 less; an oil boiler from £90 more to £260 less; LPG from £290 to £780 less; electric storage heaters from £800 to £1,200 less. The same page gives a typical installed cost of about £12,000 (Energy Saving Trust, July 2026) and a median of £12,908 for Boiler Upgrade Scheme installs in April to June 2026 (DESNZ), with half of installs between £11,128 and £15,627.

These are simple paybacks on £12,908, using the lowest and highest published savings; your quote, tariff and insulation will move them. Subtract from the net cost whatever you would pay anyway for a new boiler or heaters, and the heat pump's real extra cost falls.

Two things end on 31 March 2027: the £9,000 uplift and 0% VAT

Ofgem's property-owner page (read on 20 November 2026) lists £9,000 for air-to-water or ground source heat pumps in eligible off-gas-grid properties, available from 21 July 2026 until 31 March 2027, and 0% VAT on installation of energy-saving materials including heat pumps from 1 April 2022 to 31 March 2027. UK Energy Partnership adds that VAT rises to 5% afterwards: on £12,908 that is about £645.

The scheme is open to residential and non-residential properties in England and Wales, excluding social housing, most new builds and hybrid systems. The installer must be MCS-certified, must replace a fossil fuel or electric system, and must commission within 120 days of applying; Ofgem says the grant is taken off your quote as an upfront discount. UK Energy Partnership states that second homes and let homes qualify, but Ofgem's page does not spell this out for landlords, so confirm with your installer before relying on it.

US hosts: the federal credit ended on 31 December 2025, and never covered landlords

The IRS page on the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit says you can claim the credit for improvements made through 31 December 2025, up to $2,000 a year for a qualifying heat pump. It also says you cannot claim it if you are a landlord or other property owner who does not live in the home. For a short-let in the US, the federal credit is therefore not available for new installs, and the IRS notes that state incentives labelled as rebates may count as gross income. Check state and utility programmes directly, and run the same break-even rule with your local kWh prices.

What to do this week

If you heat with mains gas and the boiler is under 10 years old, keep it, and re-run the rule after 25 November. If you heat with LPG, oil or electric heaters, get two MCS quotes now, because installation before 31 March 2027 keeps both the £9,000 uplift (off-gas properties) and 0% VAT. Ask each installer to quote in three lines: price before grant, grant deducted, and your balance, plus the SPF they assume.

A message you can send: “We let a 3-bedroom property in England. Please quote an air-to-water heat pump with the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant shown as a deduction, state the assumed SPF and design flow temperature, confirm the property qualifies as a let home, and give installation and commissioning dates before 31 March 2027.”

Between stays, set the thermostat back: put a line for it on your checkout checklist, whether that lives in a spreadsheet, a notes app or a platform that creates a per-checkout task list for cleaners. It costs nothing, and it works with gas, LPG or a heat pump.

What is still uncertain

Three things can move the answer. The 25 November cap announcement may change the ratio. Whether electricity VAT returns after 31 March 2027 is a government decision we cannot predict. And the cost and savings figures come from a UK Energy Partnership summary of Energy Saving Trust and DESNZ data, not from your property: only an MCS heat-loss survey gives your real numbers. Frequently asked questions

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than a gas boiler in the UK in late 2026?

Only just. At the Ofgem cap of 26.32p per kWh electricity and 7.97p gas, a heat pump needs an SPF above about 2.77 to match an 84% efficient gas boiler. At an SPF of 2.93 the fuel saving is roughly £48 a year for 9,460 kWh of heat, before standing charges.

Can a landlord or Airbnb host claim the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant?

Ofgem says the scheme is open to residential and non-residential properties in England and Wales but excludes social housing, most new builds and hybrid systems. UK Energy Partnership states that homes you let qualify. Ask your MCS installer to confirm in writing before you sign.

When do the £9,000 grant and 0% VAT on heat pumps end?

Both end on 31 March 2027 according to Ofgem. The £9,000 grant applies to eligible off-gas-grid properties heated by oil or LPG and runs from 21 July 2026. After 31 March 2027, UK Energy Partnership says VAT on installs rises to 5%, about £645 on a £12,908 job.

Can US landlords claim the federal heat pump tax credit?

No. The IRS says the credit applied to improvements made through 31 December 2025 and that landlords who do not live in the home cannot claim it. State and utility rebates may still exist, and the IRS notes some state incentives can count as taxable income.
