Contractor Estimate Follow-Up: Zapier vs Make vs By Hand

The break-even math, plan prices checked on Nov. 24, 2026, and the FCC texting order that changes how you build the sequence.

In short: Automating estimate follow-ups pays off from about 6 to 8 estimates a month (3 follow-ups of 5 minutes, $60 an hour, 6 hours of setup). Make's free plan covers 25 estimates a month; your time costs more than the subscription.
Contents
Key points
  • As of Nov. 24, 2026, Zapier's pricing page lists Professional at $19.99 a month billed annually ($29.99 billed monthly) for 750 tasks; its free plan stops at 100 tasks and two-step Zaps. Make gives 1,000 free credits and sells 10,000 credits for $12 a month (Core).
  • With three five-minute follow-ups per estimate and a $60 hour, following up by hand costs $15 per estimate. Automation (6 hours of setup, 1 hour of upkeep a month) is cheaper from about 6 to 8 estimates a month.
  • The subscription is a minor line item. Your setup and upkeep time is the bulk of the cost.
  • The FCC's order released Oct. 1, 2026 lets businesses designate an exclusive way to opt out of informational texts, so any automated text step needs a working STOP path.

Automating estimate follow-ups pays off once you send roughly 6 to 8 estimates a month and are willing to spend about six hours building the sequence; below that, a calendar reminder does the same job. Above it, Make's free plan or Zapier Professional both work, and what separates a good deal from a bad one is your hourly rate, not the subscription.

When does automating estimate follow-up cost more than doing it yourself?

Use placeholder numbers and swap in yours: 25 estimates a month, three follow-ups each (day 2, day 6, day 13), five minutes per follow-up by hand (find the customer, write, send, log it), and your time valued at $60 an hour.

By hand, 25 estimates times 3 follow-ups times 5 minutes is 375 minutes, or 6.25 hours, or $375 a month. Per estimate, that is $15.

The automated version has three cost lines: the subscription, one hour a month of upkeep (a Gmail connection that expires, a template that goes out with a blank name), and setup spread over 12 months. Six hours of setup at $60, divided by 12, is $30 a month. With a free tool that totals $60 + $30 = $90 a month. With Zapier Professional at $19.99, it totals about $110.

Divide that fixed cost by the $15 it costs to follow up one estimate by hand and you get the threshold: 90 ÷ 15 = 6 estimates a month with a free tool, 109.99 ÷ 15 = 7.3, so 8, with Zapier Professional. Under that volume, the build never earns back its hours.

A second test matters more if you do not follow up today. With a $4,500 average job and a 30% gross margin (examples), each job leaves $1,350. A $109.99 monthly all-in cost is covered when follow-ups win 109.99 ÷ 1,350 = 0.08 extra jobs a month, about one extra job a year. No public study we found measures how many extra jobs follow-ups win for small contractors, so measure it yourself by comparing two quarters of closed estimates.

What do Zapier and Make charge for a three-touch sequence?

Zapier's pricing reference, updated Nov. 24, 2026, counts a "task" for each successful action. Triggers, filters, paths and the built-in delay step do not count. A sequence of three emails plus a final status update therefore uses about 4 tasks per estimate, an assumption to confirm in your first month's history. In Make, each module that runs uses one credit, including reading the spreadsheet row, while the router is free. The same sequence uses about 8 credits.

What changed in the last 60 days: the FCC's October texting order

The FCC adopted a Report and Order on Sept. 30, 2026 and released it Oct. 1, 2026 (CG Docket 02-278). It replaces the "revoke all" opt-out rule that was waived until Jan. 31, 2027. Per law firm Mintz's Nov. 16 summary, callers may now read an opt-out as covering only the category of informational texts it responded to, and may designate exclusive opt-out methods: a reply such as STOP, END, CANCEL or UNSUBSCRIBE, a key-press, or a stated website or phone number, as long as the method is clearly disclosed. The rules take effect 30 days after Federal Register publication; we did not confirm that publication date.

The same document opens a comment round on proposals including requiring two-way texting, so one-way text platforms could lose their alternative opt-out. For a contractor, this week's consequence is concrete: if an automation sends texts, it needs a reply-STOP path that stops the sequence, and the first message should say how to opt out. The order's own footnote notes that texts sent with an autodialer require prior express consent; whether a given Zapier or Make text setup counts as an autodialer is a legal question this article does not settle. Talk to a lawyer before building a text step. Email steps sit under different rules and are the lower-risk place to start.

Which three costs does the pricing page leave out?

The first is the data. A follow-up needs, for each estimate, the send date, the customer's contact and the status (won, lost, pending). If estimates live in a notebook or a sent-mail folder, the automation has nothing to read, and entering those three fields by hand stays the chore that matters. Housecall Pro's guide to quote follow-up, published by a vendor that sells this feature, lists automated scheduling as a benefit; the real work is still capturing the estimate.

The second is the silent failure. Zapier offers autoreplay for temporary errors on its paid plans, but a scenario whose email connection has expired stops without anyone noticing. Put a ten-minute monthly check in your calendar: follow-ups sent should be close to estimates sent.

The third is overage. On Zapier monthly billing, tasks beyond your quota cost 2.5 times the base rate; on Make, extra credits cost 25% more than plan credits. At 25 estimates a month you sit far below those limits. A misconfigured loop that re-sends to your whole contact list in one run is how you hit them.

What should the automation send, and what should you phone?

Recommendation: under 6 estimates a month, follow up by hand with calendar reminders on days 2 and 6. Between 6 and roughly 100 estimates, build the sequence on Make's free plan with one path: email on day 2, email on day 6, last message on day 13, stopping immediately when the customer replies or you mark the estimate won or lost. Pick Zapier Professional only if an app you depend on connects only there. If your estimating software already includes automated follow-ups, use those since you are already paying for them.

Keep the phone for big jobs. Housecall Pro's guide suggests a first follow-up 24 to 48 hours after sending, a second 4 to 5 days later and a final nudge a week after that, with two to four sentences per email. That timing is the vendor's advice, not a measured result. A practical rule: above twice your average job, the automation sends the first reminder and you call the next day.

Template for day 2, to adapt: "Hi [first name], I sent your estimate for [job] on [date]. Did you get a chance to look it over? Happy to adjust it if the scope or timing changed. [Name, phone]". It refers only to the estimate they asked for.

On the research side, a 2011 Harvard Business Review study by Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," concluded that most companies respond to online queries too slowly. We only read its abstract, it covered online sales leads rather than contractor estimates, and it is 15 years old, so treat it as a reason to respond fast, not as a benchmark.

The limit of this math: the five minutes, six setup hours and $60 hourly value are assumptions. Time ten real follow-ups with a stopwatch; if the threshold lands below 6 estimates a month, spend nothing. Information only, not legal advice, checked Nov. 24, 2026.

Frequently asked questions

How many estimates a month justify automating follow-ups?

With three five-minute follow-ups per estimate and a $60 hour, doing it by hand costs $15 per estimate. After 6 hours of setup and 1 hour of upkeep a month, the break-even is about 6 estimates a month with a free tool and 8 with Zapier Professional.

Is Zapier or Make cheaper for estimate follow-ups?

As of Nov. 24, 2026, Make's free plan gives 1,000 credits (about 125 estimates a month at 8 credits each), while Zapier's free plan allows 100 tasks and only two-step Zaps. Zapier Professional starts at $19.99 a month billed annually for 750 tasks.

Can a contractor text estimate follow-ups automatically?

Texts sent with an autodialer require prior express consent under the TCPA, and the FCC's Oct. 1, 2026 order lets senders designate an exclusive opt-out such as a STOP reply, clearly disclosed. Whether your setup counts as an autodialer is a legal question; ask a lawyer before building a text step.

When should you follow up on an estimate?

Housecall Pro suggests 24 to 48 hours after sending, 4 to 5 days after that, and a final message a week later. That is vendor guidance, not a measured result; for large jobs, phone the day after the first reminder.
