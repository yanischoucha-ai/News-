1099 Rate vs W-2 Salary: The 2027 Math

Payroll tax, health premiums, unpaid leave and non-billable time, calculated at three salary levels.

In short: In this model, matching a $90,000 salary takes about $84 per billed hour and $117,502 of revenue. Billable hours drive the largest part of the gap, so measure yours before you quote.
Contents

To match a $90,000 W-2 salary as a solo 1099 contractor, you need about $84 an hour, provided you bill 1,394 hours a year, spend $6,000 on business costs and pay a health premium equal to the employer-plan average. That is 1.95 times the $43.27 you get by dividing the salary by 2,080 hours.

The gap is not one tax. It is five separate lines, and the largest is time you can no longer charge anyone for.

Key figures
  • Self-employment tax is 15.3%, generally applied to 92.35% of net earnings (IRS Topic 554, checked November 28, 2026).
  • The 2027 Social Security wage base is $192,300, up from $184,500 (Federal Register, October 26, 2026).
  • Average 2026 employer-plan premium for single coverage: $9,804, of which workers paid $1,672 (KFF, October 2026).

Why does $43 an hour turn into $84?

The test used here is equal footing: after self-employment tax, health premium and retirement saving, the contractor should keep what the employee keeps after payroll tax and their premium share. The employee in this example also gets a 5% retirement contribution from the employer. That 5% and the $6,000 of expenses are assumptions to replace with yours.

The payroll step follows the IRS self-employment tax rules: 12.4% for Social Security up to the wage base and 2.9% for Medicare with no cap, against 7.65% withheld from an employee at this pay level. An extra 0.9% Medicare tax can apply to high earners, which none of these three profiles reaches for a single filer.

How many hours will you really bill?

The leave adjustment uses Bureau of Labor Statistics compensation data for June 2026: for full-time private-industry workers, paid leave cost $4.40 per hour worked against $36.97 in wages. Treating salary as the sum of both, leave is 10.6% of it, or about 221 of 2,080 paid hours. The non-billable share, meaning sales, proposals, bookkeeping, travel and gaps between projects, has no official benchmark in these sources. Run it three ways.

At $90,000, moving from 25% to 40% non-billable adds $21 an hour, more than payroll tax and retirement combined. Before negotiating anything, log two ordinary weeks and count the hours a client would accept on an invoice. If the work comes in small jobs, a consistent estimate follow-up affects that share more than a software discount does.

What do $60,000, $90,000 and $120,000 require in revenue?

Revenue includes $6,000 of costs, the rate assumes 1,394 billed hours, and the multiple compares it with salary divided by 2,080. Behind those rows, the profit needed after expenses is $77,491, $111,502 and $145,512, with self-employment tax of $10,949, $15,755 and $20,560.

The multiple falls as salary rises because the health premium is a fixed dollar amount. A single "double it" rule therefore underprices lower salaries and slightly overprices higher ones in this model. Expenses move the answer too: at $90,000, zero costs gives $79.98 and $15,000 gives $90.74.

Read it in reverse if you already have a rate. With the same assumptions, $60 an hour equals a salary of about $60,100, $75 equals about $78,600, and $90 equals about $97,000. A contractor charging $75 to replace a $90,000 job is short $9.29 on every billed hour, close to $13,000 a year.

Does income tax change the comparison?

Income tax is left out of the equation, so here is the check on that shortcut. The contractor can deduct half of self-employment tax when figuring adjusted gross income, as IRS Topic 554 states. Health premiums and retirement contributions may also be deductible if you qualify; the Schedule SE instructions point to Form 7206 for the health insurance deduction.

Assuming all three apply in full, the $90,000 equivalent leaves $89,321 of earnings after those deductions: $111,502 minus $7,877, $9,804 and $4,500. The employee's salary minus a pretax premium is $88,328. The difference is about 1%. At $60,000 the figures are $59,212 and $58,328; at $120,000, $119,428 and $118,328. I did not model the qualified business income deduction, state taxes, local business taxes or premium tax credits. Any of them can move your result, so have a preparer run your return before you resign. Entity choice is a separate question, covered in our sole proprietor, LLC and S-corp math.

What does the same worker cost your client?

The same BLS table puts total full-time private-industry compensation at $54.00 per hour worked, including wages, leave, supplemental pay, insurance, retirement and legally required benefits. Divided by wages plus paid leave, $41.37, that is about 1.31 times base pay. Applied to $90,000, it gives roughly $117,500, almost exactly the $117,502 of revenue calculated above from the contractor's side.

That agreement is a useful sanity check, not proof: the BLS figure is an average across occupations and firm sizes, and it excludes the self-employed. It still gives you a plain argument. An $84 quote for 1,394 hours costs a client about what an average-loaded $90,000 employee would, while the client buys no idle time. For field trades, build the rate from job costs as well, using the hourly rate that holds margin.

What changed in October, and what should you do this week?

Three dated releases affect a 2027 quote. The Social Security Administration notice raises the wage base by $7,800; for someone whose net earnings reach the new cap, the 12.4% portion rises by about $967. It does not change the three examples, which sit below both caps. The Employment Cost Index published October 30 shows private-industry compensation up 3.4% over twelve months to September, with health benefit costs up 6.4%. Indexing $84.29 by 3.4% gives $87.15. The KFF survey reports single premiums up 5% in 2026, about $470 a year on $9,804, or $0.33 per billed hour.

The deadline is health coverage. On HealthCare.gov, enroll or change plans by December 15 for coverage starting January 1; open enrollment ends January 15. State-run marketplaces may set other dates. Replace the $9,804 proxy with a real quote for your age, county and household: an individual-market premium can be far from an employer-plan average, and every $1,000 of extra annual cost adds roughly $0.75 to $0.85 per billed hour here once self-employment tax is included.

The quote to send, and the floor behind it

Hello [name], for [scope], my 2027 rate is $[rate] per hour, with an estimated [hours] hours and a cap of $[amount] unless you approve more in writing. This includes [deliverables, meetings, tools] and excludes [travel, materials, extra revisions]. Payment is due [terms], with a $[deposit] deposit before work starts. This quote is valid until [date].

Set your floor with one line: required profit plus expenses, divided by billable hours you have actually recorded. Decline or rescope work below it unless it fills hours that would otherwise stay empty and displaces nothing better. If an offer gives you one client, fixed hours and employee-like control, ask a professional whether the arrangement is properly classified before pricing it.

The limit of this analysis is its inputs. It covers one single-coverage adult, no state tax and no unemployment, disability or liability costs beyond what you put in expenses. Family coverage averaged $28,303 in the KFF survey, so rerun the premium line if others depend on your plan. Sources were checked November 28, 2026; this is information, not tax or legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

What 1099 rate matches a $90,000 W-2 salary?

About $84.29 an hour under this model: 1,394 billed hours, $6,000 of expenses, a $9,804 health premium and a 5% retirement contribution, before state taxes.

Is doubling the salary-based hourly rate a safe rule?

It is close at these assumptions, but the multiple ranged from 2.08 at $60,000 to 1.88 at $120,000, and billable hours can move the rate by tens of dollars.

What self-employment tax rate applies in this calculation?

15.3%, made of 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare, generally on 92.35% of net earnings. The Social Security portion stops at the wage base, $192,300 for 2027.

When must I enroll for January 1 Marketplace coverage?

On HealthCare.gov, by December 15. Open enrollment ends January 15, and state-run marketplaces may use different dates.
