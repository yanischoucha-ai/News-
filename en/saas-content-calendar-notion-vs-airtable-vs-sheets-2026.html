SaaS Content Calendar: Notion vs Airtable vs Sheets

Seat math for writers, reviewers and a freelancer, the Airtable Business crossover, and the columns that tie each piece to signups.

In short: If you already pay for Google Workspace, Sheets adds no subscription at 8 pieces a month. For six people, Notion Plus runs $50 a month on annual billing and Airtable Team $60 to $120, depending on whether three reviewers can comment.
Contents

Key figures (pages read November 27, 2026)

  • Airtable bills commenters on its Team plan (help center, 2026), at $20 per user per month on annual billing or $24 monthly.
  • Notion Plus costs $10 per seat per month billed annually or $12 monthly (2026), and the Free plan lists 10 guests.
  • Google Workspace Business Starter costs $7 per user per month on a one-year commitment, before tax (2026).
  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median pay for market research analysts and marketing specialists at $37.87 an hour (May 2025).

A SaaS team already paying for Google Workspace should keep its content calendar in Sheets at around 8 pieces a month: the added subscription is $0, versus $50 a month for Notion Plus and $120 for Airtable Team (annual billing) for a six-person content group. Pay for Notion when each date has to open onto its brief and draft, and for Airtable when content, campaigns and results need linked tables and steady automation.

The number that swings the bill most is the count of people who only comment.

Why does the same team cost $60 or $144 a month on Airtable?

Because seat type and billing period stack. Take this assumed team, and swap in your own: two in-house writers, three reviewers who only comment (founder, product, sales) and one freelance editor, shipping 8 pieces a month. On Airtable Team, all six are billable if the reviewers can comment: $120 a month on annual billing ($1,440 a year) or $144 month to month ($432 over three months). Set the three reviewers to read-only, which is free, and you pay for three: $60 or $72 a month.

Airtable's help page is blunt about it: “a collaborator you add as a Commenter to keep costs down still adds to a Team plan bill.” The same page says that on annual billing an added collaborator is charged for the rest of the contract, while removing one mid-cycle does not shrink the current invoice. The pricing page FAQ describes charges for users with edit permissions, which reads differently, so check the billable list in your admin panel before you commit.

When is Airtable Business cheaper than Team?

When commenters outnumber editors by more than 25%. The plan overview says self-serve Business does not bill commenters, while Team does. With E editors and C commenters on annual billing, Business ($45) costs less than Team ($20) when 45 × E is lower than 20 × (E + C), which means C is greater than 1.25 × E. With 3 editors the switch happens at 4 commenters: Team costs $140 a month and Business $135. On monthly billing it is $168 against $162.

Two conditions apply: self-serve Business requires a private email domain, and you give up the option of simply making occasional reviewers read-only on Team, which stays far cheaper if they never need to comment.

How long do the free plans actually last?

Notion's runs out in about three months for a two-or-more-member workspace shipping 8 pieces with 40-block briefs. Notion caps such a free workspace at 1,000 blocks, counts API-created content, and does not give blocks back when you delete them (there is a three-day grace period once you hit the limit). We assume each piece uses one block for its database entry plus its brief: 8 × 41 = 328 blocks a month. At 25-block briefs you get 4.8 months; at 80 blocks, 1.5. A one-person workspace gets 5,000 blocks. Inviting collaborators as guests keeps them out of the member count; a person added as a member to a free workspace joins as an owner.

Airtable Free fits our team (up to 5 editors and 50 commenters) at $0. It stops at 1,000 records per base, 100 automation runs a month and two weeks of history, and its calendar view lacks date ranges and multiple date fields. Eight rows a month will not hit the record cap soon. Automations might: five status alerts per piece already use 40 runs, and failed runs count.

Google Sheets takes up to 20 million cells or 100 MB, so size is irrelevant. Features are the constraint: timeline view and conditional notifications are limited to eligible work or school accounts, and those notifications do not reach group addresses or non-Google recipients.

What changed since October 2026, and what should you do this week?

Airtable has a new owner. Bending Spoons closed the deal in early September and wrote on October 12 that nothing changes for customers' plan, price or service, while noting that a large portion of the previous team is leaving. It also cited more than 30 improvements in three weeks, including higher automation limits. In the community thread on that update, a user wrote on October 13 that a record-limit increase expected around the second quarter had slipped; the official October 31 reply called higher limits a top priority without giving a date. A fall release announced around October 21 added Omni 2.0 on all plans, with agent audit logs reserved for Enterprise Scale.

Notion changed its AI credits on October 19: Business and Enterprise include 1,500 standard and 500 select credits per seat per month, and existing customers moved over on November 2. Free and Plus get only a limited trial. If your plan was to draft briefs with built-in AI, price Business at $20 per seat annually ($100 a month for five members), twice the Plus figure above.

  • Airtable: export the collaborator list and move people who never comment to read-only before the next renewal.
  • Notion: check block usage on a shared free workspace, or credit consumption since November 2 on Business.
  • All three: write down the renewal date and the seats you used last month.

Which tool should you pick, and when should you skip the upgrade?

Stay in Sheets if one row per piece covers your workflow and everyone who needs reminders has a Google account. Leave it when briefs scatter across dozens of files or manual follow-ups eat more than the thresholds above.

Pick Notion Plus if writers want the brief, notes and draft behind each date. Its calendar view needs a date property and can switch between properties, such as draft due and publish date. Skip it if many outside people must be full members, or if included AI is a requirement and Business breaks the budget.

Pick Airtable Team if pieces, campaigns, authors and results live in linked tables with frequent automations (50,000 records per base and 25,000 runs on Team). Skip it for a reviewer-heavy group unless those reviewers can be read-only, or run the Business math.

The subscription is a small slice of content cost. Assume 6 hours per article at $37.87, or $227.22. Notion adds $6.25 per article (2.75% of that labor) and six-seat Airtable adds $15 (6.6%). An hour saved in review is worth more than either.

Give search pages time. An Ahrefs study (May 2025, updated August 2026, on data starting in 2023, published by an SEO tool vendor) found that 1.74% of 1 million random URLs reached Google's top 10 within a year, against 6.11% in a sample of 2 million non-empty English pages. Neither rate predicts your site; they support a review at roughly six months, which the author suggests. What this comparison does not prove

It is built from vendor pricing and help pages, not from running a team on each tool, so no time savings are measured. Team shape, brief length, hours and signups are assumptions, and prices can change after November 27. A practical test: run one month in the tool you already pay for, log every manual follow-up, and upgrade only if those hours clear the threshold in the table. Frequently asked questions

Does Airtable charge for commenters?

On the Team plan, yes, according to Airtable's help center. Self-serve Business does not bill commenters, and read-only access is free. The pricing page mentions edit permissions, so confirm your billable list.

Is Notion's free plan enough for a content calendar?

For one person it allows 5,000 blocks. With two or more members the cap is 1,000 blocks, about three months at 8 pieces a month with 40-block briefs, and deleting blocks does not free capacity.

How much does a six-person content calendar cost?

In our assumed team, annual billing gives $50 a month for Notion Plus with the freelancer as a guest, $60 to $120 for Airtable Team, and $0 extra for Sheets if you already pay for Google Workspace.

How do you measure content calendar ROI?

Log hours, production cost and signups at 30, 90 and 180 days from a tagged URL, divide cost by day-90 signups, then add your signup-to-paid rate to estimate CAC payback.
